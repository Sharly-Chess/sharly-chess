# _Sharly Chess_ release notes

## General

- Each event keeps a journal of what is done to it, shown in a new Journal page: results entered, changed or cleared, pairings, byes, penalties, players, teams, tournaments and staff accounts, with when it was done, by whom (the administrator or a staff account, even one deleted since) and from where (the computer running _Sharly Chess_, another device, a screen or _Sharly-Chess.com_), the names being kept as they were at the time. The arbiters can add notes to it, filter it by tournament, kind of action, person or origin, and download it; it follows the changes as they happen, and travels with the event when it is exported unless left out (5.3.0)
- A backup taken by hand can be given a comment (5.3.0)
- More can be done from the keyboard: in a window with a split button such as "Create" / "Create and add another", Shift+Enter submits with the other choice of the button; in the players tab, the arrow keys move from row to row, Enter opens the player, Space checks them in or out, and the check-in icon can be reached with the Tab key (5.3.0)

## Tournaments

- A player who leaves an individual round-robin can be replaced, from the player form, by a newcomer who takes over their seat from a chosen round: their Berger number, their remaining opponents and their colours. The tournament keeps its rounds and its schedule, both players are ranked on the games they played, and in the tie-breaks the seat counts as a single opponent worth the points of all who held it. The Berger grid gives the seat one line, and the TRF gives the tournament as a custom round-robin with a comment for each replacement (5.3.0)
- The "Export event (SCE)" button of the warning shown before a tournament export that loses data opens the event export options, with the players included by default: the file it produced held no players, teams or pairings (5.3.0)
- A team can be pinned to a table, as a player can be pinned to a board in an individual tournament, from the team form: its match is placed on that table every round it is paired and the other matches fill the tables around it, and changing the table renumbers only the rounds paired afterwards, leaving the rounds already played alone (5.3.0)

## Players

- The players tab follows the check-ins and results entered elsewhere, on another computer, a screen or the pairings tab, without being reloaded: the rows that change are updated in place and light up briefly, and a player who no longer matches the filters leaves the table, so that the list of the players not yet checked in shrinks as they arrive. A player checked in from the tab keeps their row while its countdown runs (5.3.0)
- The rows of the players tab are lighter, so that long lists load faster (5.3.0)
- The rating check proposes to remove a national rating that no list gave, such as an _FFE_ estimate imported as a national rating, when the national list finds the player and gives them no national rating at that cadence (5.3.0)
- The players update window reminds that updating the players only updates their information, not their ratings, with a button opening the ratings update, so the ratings can be checked right after the player data is updated from a data source (5.3.0)

## Pairings

- The pairings tab follows the results and check-ins entered elsewhere without being reloaded: the boards whose result changes are updated in place and light up briefly, the players and teams checked in move between the lists, and the button to the next round appears once the round is finished. Nothing changes while the arbiter is typing, choosing, dragging or has a window open: the update waits until they have finished (5.3.0)
- Results can again be entered before the round is published, deliberately: the result window of a board of an unpublished round explains the lock and offers to unlock it, so the forfeits of a player leaving a round robin can be recorded in advance; entering a result with the keyboard on such a round opens that window instead of refusing, and the results entered stay hidden from the public until the round is published (5.3.0)
- The tournaments of an event can be paired and published at once from the All tournaments button of the pairings tab, whose badge tells how many are ready: the next round of the Swiss, Keizer and knock-out tournaments is paired, deciding what becomes of their absent players as the Pair button does, and the next round of the round robins and fixed-table tournaments is published once the rounds before it are finished. The first round, a round opening a new period and a round that needs a confirmation are still paired from the page of their tournament, and a tournament left out, such as a one-off blitz, is left out again the next time (5.3.0)

## Staff

- Choosing a person among the search results of the account form no longer fails with an error when a role has already been given for several tournaments (5.3.0)

## Screens

- A setup assistant, opened from the Create a screen menu or the Guide page, builds a display step by step: starting from what is wanted — show the check-in status, let the players check in, show the pairings by board or by player, let the players enter their results, show the standings — it asks the questions of the standard forms one at a time, offers an existing channel or creates a new one so the display can be driven from _Sharly Chess_, and for input screens covering several tournaments can create one screen per tournament behind a menu for the players to pick theirs; everything is created in one go (5.3.0)
- The screens follow the changes as they happen rather than every ten seconds: a result updates only the games and players it concerns on the pairings, result entry, players and check-in screens, the rankings are drawn again at once, and a change to a screen, such as its font size, shows straight away, as does what is sent to a channel, even one going through several screens (5.3.0)
- A screen is drawn again only when something it shows has changed: a player withdrawn from another tournament no longer redraws every screen of the event (5.3.0)
- Timers are simpler to set up and to keep up to date: each hour is either the start of a round, worded automatically ("Round 3 starts in 4 minutes"), or a moment with a label of its own, with a preview of what the screens will show; a tournament's schedule can be copied into a timer, and copied again after it changes; a quick countdown shows "Next round in 10 minutes" in one step; an hour can be postponed together with the later hours of its day; the delays read as the colours the timer goes through, and may be 0; and the screens showing a timer are chosen in the timer's own form (5.3.0)
- On a players screen shown through a display controller, tapping a player opens the result window of their game, instead of sometimes opening the window of another board of the round; a game is always found by its own identifier, so that no screen can open another board's result window (5.3.0)
- The background of image screens can use a colour extracted from the image in one click or be filled by a blurred version of the image (5.3.0)
- The screens never take a theme: whatever the theme of the application or the appearance of the system showing them, they keep their light colours, and they tell the browser so, which keeps a device that darkens web pages on its own from turning them white-on-black; they also stay readable in a browser too old to read the colours of the theme (5.3.0)
- A pairings-by-board or result-entry screen of a team tournament paired on fixed tables no longer fails with an error on the rounds where a team fielding fewer players than there are boards leaves an empty seat on the white side of a board: the seat is shown empty, as it already was on the black side (5.3.0)
- The screen setup assistant now has the channel show what it creates: a new channel shows the new menu, rotator or screen at once, and when an existing channel is picked, the assistant tells what it currently shows (or that it shows nothing) and asks whether to switch it to the new menu, rotator or screen, its display following straight away. Its last step, now titled "Perform the following actions:", lists what will be done as actions, ending with the screen shown on the channel (5.3.1)
- The screen setup assistant asks how the tournaments are shown before they are picked, the same way for every kind of screen except the input screens: one or more tournaments on one page, one tournament over several pages (turning by themselves or picked in a menu), or a page per tournament, shown in turn by a rotator named after the screens or picked in a menu. A page per tournament, offered until now only for the round-robin grids, is available for the check-in, pairings and standings screens too; the round-robin grids, which never run over several pages, are offered the other two layouts. "Create and edit" opens the form of the screen or paged screen created, or, when a screen per tournament is created behind a menu or a rotator, the edit of all those screens at once (5.3.1)
- Several screens of the same type can be edited or deleted at once: on the screens tab, tick them in their section (or tick the section to take them all) and use Edit or Delete. The edit window holds the fields the screens share, including the channels they are designed for and, when they are all paged screens, the paging fields; a field whose values differ is shown as such, and only the fields changed are applied, each screen keeping its own values for the others (5.3.1)

## Documents

- Appreciation certificates can be printed for the players of a prize category, from the place cards document, now named Place cards and certificates: every player of the category gets a certificate, whatever the number of its prizes, or only the prize winners, those of all the categories of a prize group at once if needed. Besides the player's information, the templates of the certificates offer the player's rank in the category, the number of players in the category, the performance for the categories ranked by performance, and the prize won in the category. A standard certificate template is provided (5.3.0)
- The round-robin grid is listed among the documents before the tournament is paired, so arbiters can see it is there, and both it and the round-robin pairings explain, when asked for too early, that they cannot be printed until the tournament is paired, rather than the grid being missing from the list and the pairings printing a draw the pairing can still change (5.3.0)
- The document printing every round of a round robin at once, now named Round-robin pairings, lays each round out like the per-round pairings, with the table numbers, the players with their title and rating (the teams for a team tournament) and the result once it is known, rather than a plain list of names; the per-round document is named Pairings by round, and the round robin's own pairings are edited from a Manual pairing button on the pairings tab (5.3.0)
- The list of players and the players check-in list let the arbiter choose which columns to print, each document keeping its own choice: the rating, category, gender, federation and club are shown by default, as is a column a plugin adds such as the FFE league, while the pairing number, the FIDE ID and the national ID are offered and left out unless turned on (5.3.0)
- The documents with no fields to fill in, such as the round-robin grid, the standings and the pairings, can be downloaded for a website from the new HTML menu of their header bar, which opens on hover: Complete web page downloads a page holding the document alone, without the header bar and its buttons, and HTML code downloads only the style and the content of the document, to insert into a page of the website (5.3.2)

## _Sharly-Chess.com_

- The pairings published on _Sharly-Chess.com_ show the table numbers shown in _Sharly Chess_: when a fixed board leaves the board it displaces empty, as in _FFE_ events, a player with a fixed board is shown at that board instead of at the board the game would have had without it (5.3.0)
- An event whose tournaments are held at different places on _Sharly-Chess.com_ gives each tournament its own location, imported with the event and kept in step on every synchronisation; an event held at a single place still gives that place to all its tournaments (5.3.0)

## _FFE_

- On the _FFE_ pairing sheets (Fiche d'appariement), the letter printed for each team is its fixed-table letter, the one its place in the pairing order gives it and the one the tables and the pairings use, rather than the rank of its name in the alphabet, so that once the team order is changed the letters follow it (5.3.0)
- Team competitions are no longer uploaded automatically to the team module of the _FFE_ website: an upload rewrites the whole group, deleting the match reports and teams the tournament does not have, so it is only sent when the arbiter asks (5.3.0)
- Before uploading a team competition, _Sharly Chess_ reads the group on the _FFE_ website and asks for confirmation when the upload would empty it (the tournament has no match to send while the group holds filled-in match reports, or no team while the group lists some), or when the group, not uploaded to from this tournament yet, looks like another competition (fewer than half of its teams have the name of a team of the tournament). The confirmation window names the competition, division and group, says what will be deleted, and uploads only once the name of the group has been typed (5.3.0)
- The French school championship ranks on the points before its tie-breaks: the tie-breaks imposed on the team phases now start with the match points, and the tie-break set offered for the individual Swiss tournaments with the points, both having left them out. A team tournament picks up the correction the next time it is saved; an individual tournament to which the set was applied needs the set applied again (5.3.1)

## _ChessEvent_

- The rapid and blitz estimates of the _FFE_ imported from _ChessEvent_ arrive as estimates rather than as national ratings: the _FFE_ database _ChessEvent_ reads types them N (5.3.0)
